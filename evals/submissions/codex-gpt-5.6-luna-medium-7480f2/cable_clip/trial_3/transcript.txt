Load\n  times distance levers the top of the part off its mount and concentrates stress at\n  the inside corner where the platform meets the body.\n- **Lateral load and torsion** are answered by a second mount point, not by thickening.\n  A single-mount part carrying an off-center load twists.\n- **FDM is anisotropic.** Layer bonds are the weak direction and a cantilever moment\n  peels them apart. A consistent faceted look that prints reliably beats a fillet default. The one sanctioned round-edge treatment is `crown`, for the top rim of a closed perimeter wall, and it is **asked for, never assumed**: reach for it when the user wants a rounded rim, or when a mating part genuinely requires one, and chamfer the rim like any other edge otherwise. It exists because filleting a variable-height roofline directly dies in OCCT's corner capping, not because rims want beads.\n2. **No chamfers lying in the back face or the bottom face.** The back sits against the\n   wall and the bottom is the bed-contact face. A *sloped* edge\n   arriving at the plate is the case that is not fine: its chamfer lands tilted and lays\n   a knife edge into the first layer.
